# Quasar Microlensing Lab

**▶ Open the app: [jameshhchan.github.io/quasar-microlensing](https://jameshhchan.github.io/quasar-microlensing/)**

Visualizing microlensing light curves of lensed quasars.

Stars in a lensing galaxy split each image of a lensed quasar into many micro-images. As the stars move, their network of caustics sweeps across the quasar's accretion disk and its brightness flickers. This page lets you watch that happen and see how the light curve depends on the lens model and on the size of the source. It runs entirely in your web browser; nothing to install.

## What you can do

- **Source plane:** see the magnification map with its caustics, and drag sources across it.
- **Lens plane:** watch the stars move, with their critical curves and the distorted micro-images of each source.
- **Light curve:** follow each source's magnification over time, in μ or in magnitudes, with reference curves for Gaussian sources of different sizes.
- **Sources:** choose a Gaussian, an accretion disk with an ISCO hole and a broad-line region, or your own image. Add up to four sources, each with its own light curve and light profile.
- **Lens model:** set the convergence κ, shear γ, fraction of mass in stars and stellar mass function, and draw new random star fields from a seed.
- **Tour:** click "Take the tour" on the page for a guided walk-through.

## Requirements

A recent desktop browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) with WebGL 2. A computer with a dedicated graphics card runs it most smoothly; on slower machines choose "Fast" under Ray shooting.

## Note

Built for education, not for scientific analysis. For research, please use [microcaustics](https://github.com/JFagin/microcaustics).
